\begin{abstract}
Sensitive Internet-of-Things applications execute as microservices across
edge, fog, and cloud infrastructures whose nodes differ in capacity,
jurisdiction, trust, encryption support, and hardware isolation. Scheduling
in such environments must satisfy four requirements simultaneously:
prohibited destinations must be excluded, near-term overload must be
anticipated, repeated concentration on a few nodes must be avoided, and the
final choice must retain sufficient resource headroom at commitment time.
Existing approaches usually address performance optimization, policy
enforcement, temporal prediction, or load distribution separately, leaving
no unified decision process for sensitive microservice placement. This paper presents \cage, a compliance-gated online scheduler that assigns
each requirement a distinct role in an ordered pipeline. Mandatory policies
first define the admissible node set. A calibrated temporal model then
estimates the risk of threshold crossing in the next control interval. A
fairness-aware shortlist limits repeated selection of the same destinations,
and an empirical Q95 guardrail verifies CPU and memory headroom immediately
before commitment. This ordering prevents favourable performance scores from
compensating for policy violations and separates predictive risk assessment
from final executability checking. \cage is evaluated through temporally aligned replay on the Alibaba
Microservices Trace 2021, covering 20,324 events and 12,727 nodes. The full
scheduler satisfies all enforced policy, temporal-risk, and guardrail
conditions, reduces migration relative to a matched current-state risk
baseline, and maintains millisecond-scale decision latency. The results show
that compliance, temporal awareness, fairness, and pre-commit verification
can be integrated efficiently within a single online scheduling framework.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A legality-first scheduler unifies policy, temporal risk, fairness, and guardrails.
\item Temporal history predicts unsafe next states better than current telemetry.
\item Fair shortlisting preserves full-ranking quality with millisecond-scale overhead.
\item All 462,480 seeded decisions satisfy policy, temporal-risk, and Q95 checks.
\end{highlights}
