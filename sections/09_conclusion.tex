\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \cage, a compliance-gated online scheduler for
sensitive microservices deployed across heterogeneous edge, fog, and
cloud nodes. CAGE-S organizes placement as an ordered decision process
in which policy admissibility, temporal operational risk,
fairness-aware candidate selection, and pre-commit resource verification
serve distinct and complementary roles. Mandatory requirements first
define the legal candidate space, temporal history identifies nodes
approaching an operational boundary, fair shortlisting limits repeated
destination concentration, and the Q95 guardrail verifies projected CPU
and memory headroom before commitment. The evaluation on the Alibaba Microservices Trace 2021 shows that
temporal node history improves both predictive quality and scheduler-level
decisions compared with current-state telemetry alone. On the natural
test workload, CAGE-S satisfies all enforced policy, temporal-risk, and
Q95 conditions with a 39.12\% migration rate and a 0.043\% next-state
unsafe rate. The service-balanced workload exhibits the same overall
behaviour. Robustness experiments over 30 deterministic policy overlays
and 462,480 decisions produce no policy violations, temporal-threshold
violations, guardrail violations, destination conflicts, or fallbacks.
The complete online decision core also remains well within the
30-second control interval. Overall, the results demonstrate that compliance, temporal prediction,
placement fairness, and commit-time verification can be integrated into
a single efficient scheduling pipeline. By keeping mandatory constraints
separate from optimization preferences, CAGE-S provides an interpretable
and practical foundation for scheduling sensitive microservices across
large edge--cloud infrastructures.
