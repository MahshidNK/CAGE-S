\title{Sentry: Separating Policy Admission from Predictive Microservice Placement}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
A microservice scheduler must sometimes reject a lightly loaded node because
the service is not permitted to run there. Among permitted nodes, it must
then decide whether to retain the current placement or move the service as
resource pressure changes. We present \cage, a scheduler that separates
policy admission, predicted node risk, destination reuse, and an empirical
headroom check. It retains an admissible source and otherwise searches a
shortlist ordered by previous selections, with a final admission check
before recording a destination. We evaluate the decision procedure using
Alibaba's 2021 microservice trace and synthetic policy attributes. On
11,629 held-out events across 12,727 candidate nodes, it requests migration
for 39.118\% of events, compared with 42.850\% for a current-state learned
baseline. The selected nodes' recorded next-interval unsafe rate is
0.043\%; this is an observational proxy, not an outcome of executed
migrations. Across 30 policy overlays, all 462,480 decisions pass the
configured admission checks. Mean in-memory processing time is 20.247\,ms
per natural-workload timestamp. The results identify a migration--risk
trade-off within replay; they do not establish application latency,
post-placement capacity, or production safety.
\end{abstract}
